## 1. Research Objective

Identify published capital-cost benchmarks for magnetic confinement fusion power plants and establish a transparent initial scenario framework for Proxima Fusion's Stellaris concept.

Related hypotheses: H1 (Technology Economics) and H3 (Manufacturing Scalability).

## 2. Published Fusion Cost Evidence

Araiinejad and Shirvan (2025) present a techno-economic assessment of a 350 MWe tokamak-based fusion power plant concept.

The study reports Nth-of-a-kind overnight capital-cost estimates of approximately USD 8,800–22,200/kWe and LCOE estimates of approximately USD 140–550/MWh.

Source: EC001, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apenergy.2025.126567

The results demonstrate substantial uncertainty associated with the cost of future fusion power plants, including manufacturing and regulatory assumptions.

These estimates are not directly transferable to Proxima Fusion's Stellaris reactor because of differences in reactor configuration, plant size, maturity and regional economic conditions.

## 3. Model-Cost Definitions

The first economic model uses total capital investment, fixed annual OPEX, variable OPEX, rated net electrical power, capacity factor, economic lifetime and a real discount rate.

For this preliminary exercise, the capital investment is treated as an upfront cost at time zero.

The published benchmark concerns overnight capital costs, which exclude construction-period financing. A later model extension will distinguish overnight costs from financed project capital expenditure.

Methodological references: EC002 and EC003.

## 4. Preliminary Stellaris Scenario Design

Three analyst-defined overnight capital-intensity scenarios are introduced:

- Low: EUR 8,000/kWe
- Mid: EUR 12,000/kWe
- High: EUR 18,000/kWe

These are hypothetical sensitivity cases, not company-reported costs and not converted values from the published US-dollar benchmark.

All three scenarios assume:

- Net electric output: 1,000 MWe
- Capacity factor: 85%
- Fixed OPEX: EUR 200 million/year
- Variable OPEX: EUR 5/MWh
- Economic lifetime: 35 years
- Real discount rate: 8%

Every listed value remains an analyst scenario input rather than a verified commercial Stellaris specification.

The simplified LCOE model produces approximately EUR 124, 170 and 239/MWh for the low, mid and high scenarios.

These results are conditional outputs of the assumed inputs, not forecasts of Proxima's eventual electricity-generation cost.

## 5. Interpretation

The analysis demonstrates that capital intensity can substantially influence the economics of a hypothetical fusion power plant.

However, the exercise cannot yet establish whether any specific CAPEX scenario is technically feasible or economically realistic for Stellaris.

The published ARAI benchmark provides context for the magnitude and uncertainty of fusion plant costs, but additional normalization would be required before using it to quantitatively calibrate a European stellarator model.

## 6. Remaining Calibration Gaps

The following require additional work:

1. Currency and cost-year normalization.
2. FOAK versus NOAK capital-cost assumptions.
3. Differences between tokamak and stellarator plant architecture.
4. Magnet and balance-of-plant cost breakdowns.
5. Fixed and variable OPEX reference values.
6. Realistic net rated electrical output.
7. Availability and maintenance assumptions.
8. Construction schedules and financing during construction.
9. Country-specific regulatory and construction conditions.

## 7. Implications for Research Hypotheses

**H1:** Capital cost has a material mathematical effect on LCOE. Whether stellarator operating advantages can compensate for higher investment costs remains unresolved.

**H3:** Manufacturing and regulatory assumptions contribute to the wide published fusion cost range. The effect of repeated production and component standardization will be evaluated separately.

**H2:** Partnership benefits must be evaluated through financing and risk allocation rather than inferred from plant CAPEX estimates.

## 8. Research Status

Literature benchmark and preliminary cost-sensitivity framework established.

The model is not yet fully calibrated to Proxima Fusion or the European market. No hypothesis has been confirmed or rejected.
