# Stage 3.2 — Reactor Physics and Technology Trade-offs

## Research Objective

Investigate engineering differences between the reactor concepts of Proxima Fusion, Type One Energy, Thea Energy, Commonwealth Fusion Systems, and Tokamak Energy, with emphasis on their potential influence on commercial competitiveness.

**Related hypotheses:** H1 (Technology Economics) and H3 (Manufacturing Scalability).

## 1. Preliminary Technology Comparison

| Company | Reactor concept | Main potential benefit | Main engineering challenge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proxima Fusion | Quasi-isodynamic stellarator | Steady-state operation and optimized confinement | Complex modular HTS coils and maintenance |
| Type One Energy | Optimized stellarator | Steady-state configuration | Non-planar magnet manufacturing |
| Thea Energy | Planar-coil stellarator | Simplified coil geometry | Complex field shaping and system integration |
| CFS | High-field tokamak | High-field compactness and established tokamak physics basis | Plasma current control and disruption management |
| Tokamak Energy | Spherical tokamak | Compact plasma geometry | Central-column engineering, shielding and access |

These are preliminary design trade-offs rather than proven comparative economic advantages.

## 2. Magnetic Field and Mechanical Loading

Magnetic pressure provides a simplified measure of electromagnetic loading demands:

\[
p_B = \frac{B^2}{2\mu_0}
\]

The Stellaris conceptual design reports an average magnetic field of 9.0 T and a peak conductor magnetic field of 24.9 T.

The corresponding magnetic pressure scales are approximately 32.2 MPa and 246.7 MPa, respectively.

These values should not be interpreted as actual structural stresses. The magnetic fields refer to different locations, and detailed mechanical analysis requires electromagnetic force distributions, geometry and support-system modeling.

## 3. Preliminary Economic Implications

**H1 — Technology Economics:** Steady-state operation could offer operational benefits, but commercial competitiveness will depend on net electricity output, availability, construction cost and auxiliary power requirements.

**H3 — Manufacturing Scalability:** Coil geometry, electromagnetic loads, modularization and manufacturing repeatability may influence reactor construction costs and scale-up potential.

The current evidence identifies plausible cost drivers but does not quantify their overall economic impact.
