# Stage 3.5 — Engineering-to-Economics Mapping

## Research Objective

Translate the reactor engineering and technology-maturity evidence established in Stages 3.2–3.4 into the input variables required for techno-economic modeling.

**Research focus:** Proxima Fusion's Stellaris concept.

**Related hypotheses:** H1 (Technology Economics), H2 (Commercialization Partnerships), H3 (Manufacturing Scalability).

## 1. Engineering-to-Economics Relationships

The primary relationships identified are:

- Magnet design and manufacturing requirements influence magnet-system CAPEX.
- Reactor architecture and structural engineering influence total construction CAPEX.
- Maintenance intervals and replacement durations influence scheduled availability.
- Auxiliary power requirements affect net electricity output.
- Operating availability and net rated power determine annual electricity production.
- Manufacturing standardization and learning may reduce future construction costs.
- Financing terms influence discount rates, investment burden and company-level risk exposure.

These relationships are plausible causal mechanisms, not yet calibrated cost equations.

## 2. Model Input Requirements

The preliminary techno-economic model requires:

- Plant construction CAPEX
- Fixed and variable OPEX
- Major replacement expenditure
- Net rated electric output
- Annual capacity factor
- Economic plant lifetime
- Discount rate
- Manufacturing learning assumptions

For the subsequent investment and revenue model, electricity prices, sales contracts, construction schedules and project financing must also be considered.

## 3. Evidence Gaps

Current technical publications provide several conceptual engineering specifications but do not establish a complete, independently verified commercial cost model.

Missing parameters will remain explicitly identified. They will be addressed through transparent ranges, reference studies and scenario assumptions where justified.

Conceptual design outputs will not be represented as demonstrated commercial performance.

## 4. Implications for Hypotheses

**H1:** Evaluate whether potential steady-state operation and plant availability can offset higher construction and engineering costs.

**H2:** Evaluate whether utility, industrial and financial partnerships change Proxima's commercialization risks and capital requirements under alternative contractual structures.

**H3:** Evaluate whether manufacturing learning, component standardization and repeated construction can materially reduce commercial stellarator costs.

## 5. Stage 4 Modeling Requirements

The economic analysis should:

1. Distinguish FOAK and NOAK plants.
2. Calculate discounted LCOE using consistent cost and output definitions.
3. Model project NPV separately from LCOE.
4. Distinguish gross and net electricity.
5. Include explicit maintenance and availability assumptions.
6. Use real electricity-market evidence for revenue scenarios.
7. Record assumptions and uncertainties without treating hypothetical inputs as reported company specifications.

## 6. Research Status

Engineering input mapping completed at a preliminary level. The hypotheses are not yet confirmed or rejected. Quantitative cost and revenue modeling will follow in Stage 4.
